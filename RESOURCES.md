# Strategic Software Design Resources

## Knowledge

- [John Ousterhout: A Philosophy of Software Design, second edition](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/book.php). Author's book page and edition notes; use for complexity, strategic investment, deep modules, information hiding, comments, and design alternatives. The page was inspected during setup. The linked second-edition extract is a scanned PDF, not text-extractable here; do not imply its contents or the full book have been verified from this page. Assign readings by chapter title, not unverified quotations or page numbers.
- [John Ousterhout: CS 190 Introduction, Winter 2024](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cs190-winter24/lectures/intro/). Author's public lecture notes, inspected for Lesson 2. The “Complexity” section defines complexity through difficulty understanding and modifying software, names dependencies/inconsistencies/special cases, and explains why modular design hides complexity. Use as the short primary reading for diagnosis. Lesson 2's detailed diagnostic questions and stale-rule example are instructional synthesis, not quotations from these notes. Companion [APOSD discussion outline](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cs190-winter24/lectures/aposd/) confirms the course's focus on interfaces, dependencies, information hiding, and making code obvious. The current [author book page](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/aposd.php) supplies further reading.
- [Hunt & Thomas: The Pragmatic Programmer, 20th Anniversary Edition](https://pragprog.com/titles/tpp20/the-pragmatic-programmer-20th-anniversary-edition/). Publisher page with verified table of contents and official extracts; use for orthogonality, reversibility, tracer bullets, contracts, testing, and refactoring. This edition is the course baseline.
- [Official extract: DRY—The Evils of Duplication](https://media.pragprog.com/titles/tpp20/dry.pdf). Read during setup. Distinguishes duplicated knowledge from merely similar code and describes maintenance as continuous; primary reading for Lesson 1. Independent concepts may coincidentally have the same representation.
- [Eric Evans: DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/ddd/reference/), [direct PDF](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf). Author's public pattern summaries, derived from the original book with later additions explicitly marked; definitions and contents inspected during setup. Use for ubiquitous language, model-driven design, bounded contexts, aggregates, and strategic design. This is a reference, not a replacement for the book's full explanations. Summaries licensed CC BY 4.0; attribute Evans when adapting them.
