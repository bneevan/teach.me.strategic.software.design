Follow the knowledge

Evaluate a design against an actual change, not the appearance of tidy code.

Working glossary

Change amplification
One conceptual change requires coordinated edits in several places. Investigate why; multiple changed files alone do not prove a bad design.
DRY
Give a piece of knowledge one authoritative representation. Similar text or equal values do not necessarily represent the same knowledge.
Module
Something with an interface and implementation: a function, class, package, or larger slice.
Interface
Everything a caller must know to use a module correctly, including invariants, error behavior, sequencing, and relevant performance constraints.
Information hiding
Keep a design decision inside a module so callers do not independently encode or depend on it.
Depth
Useful behavior behind an interface that is simpler to understand than the work it hides. Not a reward for extra implementation lines or an excuse to combine unrelated responsibilities.

A five-question review

  1. What business fact changed?
  2. Where is that fact independently encoded?
  3. Which similar-looking facts have different reasons to change?
  4. What is the smallest justified improvement that localizes the decision?
  5. What observable tests would reveal inconsistency or a boundary mistake?
AI instruction pattern

“Apply [specific change]. Identify every representation of [the business fact]. Preserve [independent rule]. Propose the smallest justified design change. Add behavioral evidence, show the diff, and report assumptions and checks actually run.”

Avoid false shortcuts

  • A shared constant hides a number, but may leave callers responsible for interpreting the rule.
  • Fewer files, shorter functions, and more abstractions are not automatic improvements.
  • A shared UI preview does not replace authoritative server enforcement.
  • Independent domain meanings should not be unified merely because values match today.
Primary source: Hunt & Thomas, official DRY extract. Further study: Ousterhout, A Philosophy of Software Design, chapters on complexity, modules, and information hiding. Definitions and review prompts are course paraphrases/synthesis, not quotations. Ask your teaching agent to apply this checklist to a real diff.
